\section{Two mechanics}
\label{sec:kpcfixed:operations}

SOURCE and MERGE define $C_q(x)$ of every handle by recursion in dependency
order. Their cases depend only on the creating event's consumed handles.
Validity uses exact enforcement under \zcref{assum:kpcfixed-model:realization}.

\begin{operation}[SOURCE]
\label{op:kpcfixed-operations:source}
An event $e$ with $I_q(e)=\varnothing$ that creates a handle
$s=O(e)_0\in K_q$ is a SOURCE. The handle itself identifies the new record
$r_q(s)=(q,s)$ and receives $C_q(s)=\{r_q(s)\}$.
The empty condition counts handles of this same triple: the
event consumes zero handles of $K_q$, not necessarily zero inputs.
It may consume inputs that are not handles, including those that fund it.
Under the assumptions of \zcref{thm:kpcfixed-invariants:key-isolation},
a handle of another triple cannot be among them.
Call $s$ the SOURCE handle of that record. Its fresh identity binds its
creating event, so no other event introduces that record.
If a published reader rule requires attributable destruction for a
record, that destruction belongs in this SOURCE event. A MERGE or an
event creating no handle introduces no record to which it could be credited.
\end{operation}

\begin{operation}[MERGE]
\label{op:kpcfixed-operations:merge}
If $I_q(e)\ne\varnothing$, continue-first supplies $s=O(e)_0\in K_q$.
MERGE assigns $C_q(s)=\bigcup_{x\in I_q(e)}C_q(x)$ and introduces no record.
The consumed handles' covenant places no condition on the event's other
outputs.
\end{operation}

\begin{proposition}[Merge surplus]
\label{prop:kpcfixed-operations:merge-surplus}
A MERGE consuming $n\ge 1$ handles of $q$ retains one deposit $v$ of $A$ at
its successor and leaves $(n-1)v$ unconstrained by the covenant, without
introducing records or constraining that surplus's destination.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each of the $n$ handles qualifies against the same constant pair $A,v$.
Under \zcref{assum:kpcfixed-model:realization}, every continuation clause
requires the same single output zero to qualify
against that pair. Thus the handles supply $nv$ and the sole required
successor retains $v$. No clause constrains another output or demands
preservation of each deposit; MERGE assigns only the union of input sets.
The difference is $(n-1)v$. Underlying validity, fees and other inputs'
conditions remain separate constraints, so this is no promise of profit
or sufficient fee funding.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Record sets and classification]
\label{def:kpcfixed-operations:classification}
Every event $e$ creating a handle $s\in K_q$ satisfies exactly one of these
two cases, with the defining recurrence
\[
 C_q(s)=
 \begin{cases}
 \{r_q(s)\},&\text{if }I_q(e)=\varnothing,\\
 \displaystyle\bigcup_{x\in I_q(e)}C_q(x),&\text{if }I_q(e)\ne\varnothing.
 \end{cases}
\]
The empty case is separate because a union over no consumed handles would
be empty. Instead, the definition starts one record where there is no
provenance to inherit.
An event creating no handle of $K_q$ has no set to define and cannot
consume a handle of $K_q$, by continue first. This classifies every event of every valid
history without imposing any further validity condition.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Continuation]
\label{def:kpcfixed-operations:continuation}
When an event consumes a handle $x$, its first output is the
\emph{successor} of $x$. Write $x\preceq y$ when $y$ is reached from $x$
by zero or more successor steps.
\end{definition}

Record sets and successors are derived from $H$ and are not carried by
handles. The system has no counter or accumulator and no designated head or terminal.
The pair is a membership constant, not a measure of the record set or
an accumulated magnitude; no depth is carried either.
The system defines no burn or destruction law and is separate from the
attestation's Burn and Clear operations; neither is imported into the
covenant or its recurrence.

\begin{remark}[Reader folds]
\label{rem:kpcfixed-operations:reader-folds}
Meaning layered on the history beyond membership and record sets is a
reader's fold over authenticated history: a reader-chosen rule processes
events and combines their data with its accumulated result. Such a fold
may read any data of that history, including a message beside a handle,
destruction or burn evidence, value accounting, or events of another
protocol layered on the history. These meanings and their rules are
neither enforced by the covenant nor defined by this paper. The covenant
fixes only the spending condition of outputs bearing its program.
Membership and record sets are this paper's definitions over the history,
and the paper proves what follows from them. Two readers choosing different
folds may obtain different results without disagreeing about the covenant.
A fold requiring attributable destruction for a record is subject to the
rule in \zcref{op:kpcfixed-operations:source}. Reading other events cannot
create a record or change its contents: only SOURCE introduces a record
(\zcref{thm:kpcfixed-invariants:source-provenance}), whose identity binds
that event's immutable contents. A destruction or burn reading is thus an
instance of a reader's fold, consistent with the absence of a burn or
destruction law stated above.
\end{remark}
