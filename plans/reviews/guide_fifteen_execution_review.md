# Guide 15 execution review — the chartered Phase-7 execution guide

## §1. The question restated

The subject is `plans/guides/guide_fifteen.md` as chartered above `0.7.0-dev`: an execution guide that supersedes the Guide 15 concept as execution direction, closes inherited Phase-6 debt and the concept's preflight in Wave 0, carries a second contract through the compiler in Wave 1, and delivers burn, public ASH and clear in Waves 2 to 14. Its governing shape is one chain of gated waves:

```text
backlog entry record
  -> Wave 0   inherited debt closure and preflight      gate:guide15-exec:wave0
  -> Wave 1   second contract, destruction lineage      gate:guide15-exec:wave1
  -> Waves 2-13  burn, ASH, clear (concept Waves 1-12)
  -> Wave 14  Phase-7 gate and handoff                  gate:phase7:exit
```

The question is whether that chain is an executable plan: whether its order holds on its merits, whether each gate can be decided pass or fail from a gate record, whether the guide carries its sources without dropping or weakening an obligation, whether any sentence claims more than its evidence, and whether the text is ready for a formal rewrite in which every paragraph stands under a genre head and every tree fact is cited by label. The review therefore asks five things:

1. whether the order (debt and preflight, then the second contract, then the concept's waves) holds against its strongest objections;
2. whether every bite and wave gate is decidable from typed sources or a gate record;
3. whether the register, the twelve dispositions, the concept's waves, the exemplar study's requirement set and the inherited non-claims are carried faithfully;
4. whether any sentence states a plan as a result, a disposition as an observation, or a rebuilt candidate as inherited; and
5. what a rewrite must split, cite and type, and which findings it may not settle on its own because they change positions, scope or order.

Overall judgment: the guide is a careful, evidence-bound charter whose figures recount exactly at this base and whose register reproduces the admission study row for row. Its weaknesses are structural rather than factual. Its own opening rule makes burn and clear depend on the second contract, which its change statement says does not happen; Wave 0 has no exit for an observable obligation that fails on its merits, and one such obligation (positive-floor relayability) silently requires a new sponsored maturity form and a ruling the guide does not name; several position choices sit inside bites with no record that accepts them; and three closure words (RETIRED, "no missing node", "independent") carry more weight than their definitions. The first of these blocks opening Wave 1 as chartered. None blocks opening Wave 0.

## §2. The guide's actual design as read here

### 2.1 Declarations, entry and the register

The header keeps phase entry and guide entry apart, as the admission study's Alternative B proposed: the Phase-7 card retains its two entry conditions (`gate:phase6:exit`) on its amended assessment and (`sec:public-opening:result`), while the guide's Entry adds the accepted Phase-4 compact-ASH candidate at its recorded scope, "subject to the entry register below". The card's status, the backlog's status header, next-gate line and phase row, the roadmap's preamble and current-phase line, the plans README and the phase index all name Phase 7 as current and Wave 0 as the next gate (`sec:phase7:change-statement`, `phase:roadmap:burn-clear`).

The register (`tab:guide15-exec:preflight`) carries the admission study's 65 entry rows, E01 to E65 in the study's source order, grouped by a primary preflight question, with columns for evidence, finding status, execution status, blocker, required exit and class. Finding status is the study's standing qualified "at audited scope"; execution status is OPEN for every row; the required exit names a Wave-0 bite and, where applicable, a later wave. Twelve controlling dispositions answer the concept's twelve questions: five are taken in the charter's own words (explicit-only ASH representation, normalized-first private burn, the clear assignment, event-role separation, the internal query default) and seven are assigned to bite 0.7 or a later wave.

The semantic-entry contradiction the admission study found is resolved by separation: known model law and field ownership are Wave-0 obligations, typed compiler-facing burn and clear declarations are Wave-2 obligations, and the two DOES NOT HOLD rows keep their finding while receiving that execution disposition (`gate:guide15:semantic-entry`). This is the standard split between a language's source semantics and the typed intermediate declarations a compiler consumes, and it removes the circular prerequisite.

### 2.2 Wave 0

Wave 0 opens on a backlog entry record that names the pinned Elements toolchain on the node of record, the clean tree, the target and deployment binding, the retained compact and maturity candidates, the native route, public fixtures and evidence locations (`task:guide15-exec:wave0`). Its seven bites close the four inherited carriers: 0.1 verifies the provisioned target and records entry; 0.2 refits operation regions (`T11-064`'s structural half); 0.3 runs the closure-exact audit (`T11-063`); 0.4 closes the compact negative half and the Guide-13 requests (`T4-009`); 0.5 disposes all 128 outstanding Safety rows, recounts relation links and disposes the five conditional §23 conjuncts (the rest of `T11-064`); 0.6 closes test-performance measurement (`T11-110`); 0.7 closes the twelve preflight questions and publishes the baseline. The exit (`gate:guide15-exec:wave0`) is eight bullets of figures and closures, with every later wave's figures compared against the recorded baseline (`rule:guide15-exec:baseline`).

### 2.3 Wave 1

Wave 1 restates the exemplar study's contract faithfully: BURN pairs a minimal-value claim token at output 0 with a positive explicit native OP_RETURN destruction at output 1 carrying one opaque 32-byte address; MERGE consumes covenant outputs and produces output 0 under the same script, checking nothing else; a reader reconstructs settled records from token ancestry; a consumer publishes which settled records it accepts, under A(previous) ⊆ A(this) ⊆ L(this) (`task:guide15-exec:wave1`). Seven bites measure six target obligations (1.0), choose the realization home and declare the contract (1.1), compile plans (1.2), emit forms and authenticated recurrence (1.3), link and construct (1.4), build vectors and six evidence families (1.5), and re-execute final forms and record which seams moved (1.6). The nine study decisions are assigned to bites. The exit (`gate:guide15-exec:wave1`) excludes any import of attestation law into the covenant.

### 2.4 Waves 2 to 14 and the baseline rule

Each later wave cites its concept counterpart (`task:guide15:wave1`) through (`task:guide15:wave13`), repeats its deliverables and suggested commit, adds a gate sentence, and states what Wave 0 and Wave 1 supply as inherited inputs (`sec:guide15-exec:remaining-waves`). Wave 14 names the Phase-7 exit gate (`gate:phase7:exit`) without a substitute. The baseline rule makes a wave open only on its predecessor's exit, requires every gate record to state its figures beside the Wave-0 baseline, and treats a regression as a finding requiring disposition before advancement (`rule:guide15-exec:baseline`).

## §4. Findings, ranked

### F1 — CONCEPT-BLOCKER: the opening rule makes burn and clear depend on the second contract, which the change statement says does not happen

**Guide text.** §6 (`rule:guide15-exec:baseline`): "A wave opens only on the previous wave's exit gate". §5: "Every wave opens only after its predecessor's exit". The Wave-1 exit (`gate:guide15-exec:wave1`) requires that "A second contract is compiled and executed through the same pipeline" with all six evidence families and measurements bound to the final subjects. Wave 2's inherited inputs: "Wave 1 supplies the tested architecture-to-realization boundary". The header's Required result adds "with a second compiled contract carried through the same pipeline as independence evidence". The change statement (`sec:phase7:change-statement`): "with no change to phase order or dependencies; inherited debt and preflight close in Wave 0, the second contract runs in Wave 1, and burn, ASH, and clear follow in Waves 2–14". §7: "no phase dependency or normative version changes through this charter".

**Evidence.** The Phase-7 exit gate's ten bullets mention no second contract (`gate:phase7:exit`), and the roadmap's dependency rationale lists none (`rule:roadmap:dependencies`). The exemplar study's decision 6 framed admission as a cost requiring "an explicit scope and dependency decision". The 1.0 gate states that "unresolved forms block the consuming bite". A search of the guide for blocking, abandonment and stop routes finds one, the 0.1 "blocking finding"; Wave 1 has none.

**Gap.** Read together, Waves 2 to 14 cannot open until the second contract compiles, executes and passes its six evidence families on the pinned target, and Wave 14 is where the phase gate is recorded; Phase 7's exit, and through (`rule:roadmap:sequence`) every later phase, therefore depends on target facts that concern only the second contract: relay of a positive native OP_RETURN with a 32-byte datum (measurement b), replacement policy (c) and a faithful recurrence (e). The dependency is material as well as sequential, because Wave 2 builds the attestation's declarations on the realization home chosen for the second contract (decision 1). Fail-closed gating under (`rule:roadmap:laws`) stops a contract on its own unresolved requirements; here an unresolved requirement of one contract stops the other. The change statement's dependency head is contradicted by the guide's own opening rule, and the second contract was admitted on that head. This blocks opening Wave 1 as chartered; it does not touch Wave 0, which neither depends on nor feeds the second contract except through the shared target.

**Smallest change.** Either (a) keep the chain and restate the dependency head of the change statement under (`rule:roadmap:change`): burn and clear delivery depend on Wave 1's exit, with the reason the guide already gives (seams settle before burn and clear depend on them) and the consequence for later phases; or (b) give Wave 1 a typed failure exit, a recorded independence finding naming the seam or target fact that stopped the contract, on which Wave 2 opens, with decision 1 either taken or explicitly deferred so that Wave 2 does not build on an unchosen home.

**A disposition must decide** whether the second contract is a precondition of the attestation's Phase-7 delivery or evidence carried beside it.

**Bears on:** substance (ordering and dependency).

### F2 — DESIGN-RISK: Wave 0 has no exit for an observable obligation that fails on its merits, and conjunct 7 needs a sponsored maturity form and a ruling the guide does not name

**Guide text.** Bite 0.5, conjunct 7: "Observe positive-floor relayability and the selected sponsored forms on the pinned target, with exact fees, policy, subjects, and acceptance/readback evidence". Its closure carrier: "`T12-005` closes the remaining `T11-064` obligations, including Wave 11's C1 and positive-floor conditions". Its gate: "observable obligations are performed". §3: "failure to identify or reproduce the provisioned target prevents opening and cannot be converted into a typed retirement of observable work". The 0.1 gate: "the existing accepted control reproduces or a blocking finding is recorded".

**Evidence.** The Phase-6 rulings define C1 as "the sponsored STATE builder", which "depends on `T11-064` and a ruling revisiting ruling 58"; C2, the sponsored native ceremony, depends on C1 and a provisioned node; C3, the sponsor rows' retyping, depends on C1 for eleven rows and on `T11-064` for three (`rule:phase6:wave11-rulings`, `sec:phase6:wave11-findings`). Positive-floor relayability "belongs to a fee-paying form under Wave 11's C1 and to deployment" because the sponsorless candidate declares no fee. The tree agrees: `packages/transaction/src/state_finalize.rs` fixes `FeeRoleAbsent` and `SponsorRegionAbsent` among the maturity candidate's finalized facts, and `packages/transaction/src/state_construct.rs` refuses sponsored request forms with `SponsoredMaturityFormHasNoCarrier`. The guide names neither the ruling nor the builder.

**Gap.** Closing conjunct 7 as written requires a ruling that revisits ruling 58, a new fee-paying sponsored maturity transaction form (a new candidate inside the wave whose baseline records "the exact candidates"), and a native ceremony for it. If that ruling declines sponsored STATE forms, positive-floor relayability has no fee-paying form to observe; the guide forbids retiring observable work and states no other route, so Wave 0 cannot exit. The only failure route in the wave is 0.1's "blocking finding", and the guide does not say what it blocks. The Phase-6 closeout met the same kind of situation with an amended gate that narrowed each condition to what the record evidenced and left the remainder as its own figure (`sec:phase6:exit-assessment`); the guide neither adopts nor excludes that route.

**Smallest change.** Name C1, C2, C3 and the ruling on ruling 58 as 0.5 obligations in their order; state what a recorded 0.1 blocking finding blocks; and state the route for an observable obligation that a ruling removes, either a transfer to a named later carrier with the conjunct kept CONDITIONALLY MET, or an explicit statement that Wave 0 then stays open.

**A disposition must decide** whether Wave 0 may exit with an observable obligation transferred to a named carrier, or must stay open until every observable obligation is performed.

**Bears on:** substance (scope and exit).

### F3 — DESIGN-RISK: position choices sit inside bites with no record that accepts them

**Guide text.** Dispositions 1, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 11 assign recording, selection or derivation to bite 0.7. Register exits include E33 "accept synthetic origin with explicit non-claims or record an accepted migration prerequisite", E41 "select exact owner/sponsor profiles and their bindings", E28 "disposition intended equality or explicit class transitions" and E62 "select the exact compact bundle". Bite 0.4: "Remedy the three requests". Bite 0.6 closes `T11-110` "as DONE or formally closed". The 0.7 gate: "All twelve questions have explicit dispositions".

**Evidence.** The admission study placed several of these at admission: the burn-record carrier ("Admission must choose the chain-public encoding and recovery boundary"), the generation transition ("Needs an admission decision") and constructor continuity ("Admission must require exact equality ... or explicitly charter class transitions"). The Guide-13 requests are addressed to "whichever later guide takes up the negative half" and ask for guide-level rules: a relation, mutation class and boundary per negative row; a first-party discharge condition for the negative half or an explicit out-of-scope declaration for its 18 first-party rows; and an ABI-validation entry point (`plans/guides/guide_thirteen_feature_requests.md`). Phase 6 recorded such choices as numbered rulings on its card (`rule:phase6:wave13-rulings`), and `T11-110` asks that its first swiftness bite be "ruled and landed".

**Gap.** The 0.7 gate tests that a disposition exists, not that it was accepted, so a bite can choose the burn-record carrier, accept synthetic origin, select signing profiles or settle the negative-half discharge rule under its own gate, and the Wave-0 exit records the choice as preflight closure. Of the thirteen UNDECIDED rows, four have exits decided by a run outcome (E02, E17, E26, E27); the other nine are decisions or design acts. Guide 15 is the guide that takes up the negative half, yet it carries none of the three requested rules itself.

**Smallest change.** Mark each position-bearing disposition with the record that accepts it (a numbered ruling on the Phase-7 card, as Phase 6 used), carry the three Guide-13 rules in the guide or name the ruling that will, and add a Wave-0 exit bullet requiring an acceptance record for every position disposition.

**A disposition must decide** which Wave-0 dispositions are positions requiring an acceptance record and which are findings a bite may record on its own evidence.

**Bears on:** substance (decision process and scope).

### F4 — DESIGN-RISK: the "no missing node" rule is a test of wording, and the exit carries a narrower form than the bite

**Guide text.** Bite 0.5's non-answer gate: "Each of the 35 typed non-answers is reconsidered against the provisioned target and exact claim; no final reason may name a missing node, absent provisioning, or unperformed observable experiment." The exit: "All 206 Safety rows are answered or retyped with exact reasons naming no missing node". `T12-005`: "no missing-node final reason". §6: "a retyped row never becomes an observed refusal merely because it leaves an outstanding bucket".

**Evidence.** A row is a typed non-answer exactly when its boundary names no refusing layer (`classify` in `packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs`), and the 35 stand on five typed reasons (§2.5). None of the five names a node.

**Gap.** The exit bullet, which is the gate of record, and its backlog carrier would be met for all 35 non-answers without any work, since no existing reason names a node. The bite's broader clause would catch the nine AcceptanceAwaitsRelayAdmissibility rows (an acceptance under the deployed policy is an observable experiment) and, once the refit lands, the three SponsorArmAwaitsRegionScoping rows; but the bite clause is not what the exit tests. "Reconsidered" has no output a record can show. A rule stated over the words of a reason is met by renaming the reason.

**Smallest change.** State the exit bullet with the bite's full clause, and make finality a typed property of the reason: each final non-answer reason states why no observation can exist (impossibility, a claim outside the admitted contract, a property of the build, an answer held by other admitted evidence), and the census counts rows by that property beside the bucket figures.

**A disposition must decide** which of the five existing reasons are final (TotalityAnsweredByLandedNonceEvidence and PropertyOfTheBuildRatherThanAFixture point to evidence held elsewhere) and which are waiting reasons Wave 0 must discharge.

**Bears on:** substance (what the Wave-0 exit admits).

### F5 — DESIGN-RISK: RETIRED is a new standing without a definition, and the NUMS residual is already a typed conditional claim

**Guide text.** The 0.5 gate: "Every one of the five conjuncts is MET or RETIRED as a named non-claim with a reason". The exit: "individually MET or RETIRED as stated non-claims with reasons, preserving the historical NOT PASSED verdict at its original scope". Conjunct 5: "RETIRE the NUMS discrete-log impossibility claim with its reason rather than treating another rejection as its proof". The §2 table: "RETIRED as a non-claim because finite target refusals cannot prove a discrete-log impossibility". §7: "no observation can discharge the NUMS discrete-log impossibility claim".

**Evidence.** The §23 table's standings are MET, CONDITIONALLY MET and OUTSTANDING, and its conjunct-5 cell already states "The alternative MET reading treats the recipe as outside these bytes and the discrete-log limit as a non-claim" (`sec:phase6:gate-record`). The tree types the residual: `StateInternalKeyPolicy` in `packages/tapscript/src/state_constructor.rs` recomputes the internal key as the SHA-256 of the uncompressed generator and refuses any other key, carries `InternalKeyPolicy::UnspendableWithResidualDiscreteLogAssumption`, and states its residual as "discrete-log hardness and SHA-256 preimage resistance; no proof that a key path is impossible". No artifact claims impossibility.

**Gap.** The argument is correct: a finite set of refusals cannot establish that no one knows a discrete logarithm. But the claim being retired is one the tree never makes; what stands is a conditional claim under two named assumptions whose derivation is checked first-party. Reading conjunct 5 as RETIRED suggests that key-path unspendability is abandoned, while bite 1.3 relies on it ("exclude alternative spend paths ... including a usable key path, while retaining the stated NUMS non-claim"). RETIRED is also undefined at conjunct level: conjunct 5 has three parts (the NUMS residual, the wrong-control recipe, `wrong-static-subtree`) and no rule composes their standings, and nothing in the wording confines RETIRED to residuals no observation can reach.

**Smallest change.** Define the standing (a conjunct residual restated as a named assumption or a claim outside the admitted contract, with its argument, the conjunct's observable remainder standing separately), forbid it wherever a part is observable, and state the NUMS part as met under the typed assumptions, citing the derivation check.

**A disposition must decide** which conjunct residuals may close as named assumptions or non-claims, and whether those closures adopt the gate record's existing alternative MET readings.

**Bears on:** substance (closure semantics).

### F6 — DESIGN-RISK: independent chain observation is both an inherited non-claim and a Wave-0 obligation, and "independent" is undefined

**Guide text.** §2: "The inherited non-claims are no production custody, deployment, calibration, release, independent chain observation, or general theorem beyond the finite admitted corpus". The §2 table: "Independent chain observation absent | 0.5, `T12-005` | Obtain an independent observation on the pinned target and exact declared environment". §7: "the lack of independent chain observation is an inherited entry limitation that bite 0.5 must discharge for its exact observed subject". Conjunct 11: "from independently observed transactions"; conjunct 13: "Have an unrelated process reconstruct the successor from independently observed public chain data with its declared import boundary".

**Evidence.** Conjunct 13's condition reads "The process has a pinned import boundary and consumes archived mined readback; it does not independently observe a chain" (`sec:phase6:gate-record`), and the exit assessment lists "an independent observation of a chain" among what is not claimed (`sec:phase6:exit-assessment`). The entry obligation places the pinned toolchain on the node of record, "provisioned by the loop's own account under its own directory".

**Gap.** §2 lists the limitation as a non-claim, while the §2 table and §7 make it an obligation. Both can hold (an obligation for the exact observed subject, a non-claim beyond it), but the text never states the split. The larger gap is the criterion: the inherited limitation concerns the observer's independence from the producer's archive, and the guide does not say what makes an observation independent when producer, node and observer run under one account against one chain. A separate process reading the same node removes the archived readback but shares the node, the chain and the account.

**Smallest change.** Move the limitation out of the §2 non-claim list into the table's obligation form, as §7 already treats it, and state the independence criterion as a checkable property: which state the observer may not share with the producer, and which import boundary it reads.

**A disposition must decide** the independence criterion that conjuncts 11 and 13 must meet on the node of record.

**Bears on:** substance (what Wave 0 must observe).

### F7 — DESIGN-RISK: the order's carrier argument is contradicted by disposition 5

**Guide text.** §1: "Its measured carrier forms supply concrete precedent for the burn-record carrier question without choosing an attestation record law by analogy (`q:guide15:burn-record-carrier`)." Disposition 5: "bite 0.7 selects a canonical, publicly recoverable carrier with exact association and ordering; Wave-1 form measurements are later regression evidence and cannot substitute for this preflight disposition". Bite 0.7: "later OP_RETURN measurements can challenge the selected form but cannot backdate its proof". Waves 4 and 6 list Wave 1's carrier measurements as precedent.

**Evidence.** Wave 0 precedes Wave 1 under the opening rule (`rule:guide15-exec:baseline`), so the carrier is selected before any Wave-1 measurement exists.

**Gap.** §1 counts among the benefits of running the second contract early a precedent that disposition 5 forbids the carrier decision from using. The precedent can only arrive after the selection and challenge it, and the guide states no procedure for a challenge: no trigger figure and no reopening of 0.7. One of the order's seven arguments therefore does not support the order.

**Smallest change.** Either delete the §1 carrier sentence, or state that the 0.7 carrier selection is provisional and is confirmed or reopened at Wave 4 against the Wave-1 measurements, with the trigger named.

**A disposition must decide** whether the burn-record carrier is selected before or after the second contract's carrier measurements.

**Bears on:** substance (ordering).

### F8 — DESIGN-RISK: Wave 1 does not carry the second contract's whole requirement set

**Guide text.** Wave 1 "carries the contract in `plans/drafts/destruction-lineage-exemplar-study.md` §§2–6 and §9". Bite 1.5's ancestry evidence "covers a single token, many tokens, side-pile sweep, combined BURN/MERGE, multiple successive points in one block, shared ancestors, duplicate traversal, and missing ancestors". §7: "historical availability, and finality remain bound to measured subjects and published assumptions". The header excludes "production deployment".

**Evidence.** The exemplar study's §3 states thirteen requirements: eight that hold by construction, three that hold by publication (bootstrap capacity, unit cost, operator accrual) and two that hold by consumer rule. It states that "The three publication standings require an actual deployment statement" and that "A deployment publishes settlement depth, the assumptions under which final pins are not revised, historical data availability and the applicable head-conflict policy together". Its §2 requires a reader to verify ancestry "with inclusion proofs", which "must still bind the point and ancestry to the selected history".

**Gap.** No bite, gate or exit bullet produces the three publication statements or the deployment publication set, and the ancestry evidence family omits inclusion proofs. The two consumer-rule standings are carried (decision 8 and consumer-rule evidence). Because the guide excludes production deployment, the publication standings have no home: they are neither delivered as candidate statements nor carried as non-claims, so the Wave-1 exit can pass with three of the contract's thirteen requirements unaddressed.

**Smallest change.** Add to 1.5 or 1.6 a deliverable that writes the publication statements as candidate fixture artifacts bound to the pinned target (bootstrap zero with its reason, unit cost, operator accrual, settlement depth, finality and availability assumptions, and the conflict policy measurement (c) establishes), or list them in §7 as non-claims with the reason; add inclusion proofs to the ancestry family.

**A disposition must decide** whether the publication standings are Wave-1 deliverables in candidate form or non-claims of Phase 7.

**Bears on:** substance (Wave-1 scope).

### F9 — DESIGN-RISK: Wave 1 is ordered so that its cheapest informative failure comes after its costliest step

**Guide text.** Bite 1.0 performs six target measurements "before selecting stable declarations or ABI bounds". Bite 1.1 then chooses "the semantic home from the study's three options using measured and read evidence". Decision 4 at 1.0: "Choose measured N as an ABI construction bound". Bite 1.6: "changed bytes, control depth, funding, input bound, or policy invalidate any unqualified transfer of prototype observations".

## §5. Open questions

1. Is the second contract a precondition of the attestation's Phase-7 delivery, or evidence carried beside it? For a precondition: seams that move for the second contract move before burn and clear depend on them, which avoids rework, and Phase 7 then accepts the compiler as general rather than as fitted to one contract. For evidence beside it: the roadmap accepts the compiler by the attestation exemplar, the phase gate names no second contract, and a target policy fact about OP_RETURN relay should not be able to halt redemption, settlement and release. (F1)
2. When an observable Wave-0 obligation fails on its merits, does the wave stay open or narrow its gate? Staying open keeps the fail-closed law literal and prevents debt from becoming a non-claim by fatigue. Narrowing, as Phase 6 did, records exactly what was evidenced and moves the remainder to a named carrier, which keeps the phase able to proceed. (F2)
3. What record accepts a position taken inside a bite? A numbered ruling makes every position visible and separately reviewable, at the cost of a decision step per bite. Acceptance by the bite's own gate keeps the wave moving but lets a design choice enter the baseline as preflight closure. (F3)
4. What makes a chain observation independent on a node run under the producer's own account? A separate process with its own import boundary removes the archived readback, which is the inherited limitation's literal text. A separate node or chain view removes shared state as well, which is what independence usually means, but may not exist on the node of record. (F6)
5. Is the burn-record carrier selected before or after the second contract's carrier measurements? Before keeps preflight complete and prevents a record law being chosen by analogy. After lets the attestation's carrier rest on measured relay and data-output facts instead of reasoning alone. (F7)
6. Are the second contract's publication standings Wave-1 deliverables in candidate form, or non-claims of Phase 7? As deliverables, the contract's thirteen requirements are all exercised. As non-claims, Phase 7 keeps its exclusion of production deployment intact, and the standings wait for a deployment that states them. (F8)
7. Does every inherited carrier gate new semantics? Gating all of them gives one clean baseline and no interleaved debt. Gating only those with a semantic dependency lets test-performance and compact-negative work proceed beside the second contract, with their figures joining the baseline when they close. (F14)
8. Does the concept's exit checklist bind the guide's final wave? Binding it keeps identity-drift and byte-drift checks that the phase gate lacks. Leaving it unbound keeps one gate of record, the card's, and avoids two final gates that can disagree. (F11)
9. Is gate wall time a baseline figure whose regression is a finding? Including it makes a slower gate visible beside the semantic figures. Excluding it keeps the baseline to semantic and evidence figures, which a node's load cannot move. (F12)
10. Does the first swiftness bite land in Wave 0? Landing it meets `T11-110`'s own criterion. Deferring it keeps the debt wave to evidence work and lets the register close on its measurements, with the swiftness bite carried by a new row. (F13)
