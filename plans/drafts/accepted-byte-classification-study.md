# Study: the accepted-byte row classification and its bites

Status: a design study over the tree at `0.6.249-dev`, written because `T11-111`'s row text names a classification the tree cannot carry as written. Every figure below is recounted from the source or read from the assertion that pins it; nothing here was obtained by running a test, and no line of production code is changed by this file. It answers nine design questions as numbered decisions, leaves two open, and manufactures the four bites that implement the decisions.

## 0. In one breath

The accepted archive answers exactly one row of the §16 matrix — §16.1's sponsorless positive row, whose question is an acceptance — so `NativeAcceptanceObserved` displaces `NativeDeclaredBoundaryObserved` there and `ConstructorContinuityObserved` answers no row at all, because the report's entries are attributed to that same row, the continuity mutants are experimental by type, and the only row whose question names the constructor projection term demands four terms of which two have no producer; the acceptance reaches the row from the pinned accepted corpus rather than from an arbitrary established report, the projector gains a verified acceptance claim so that the report over accepted bytes stops rendering an outstanding obligation, and the disclosed zero fee floors ride as a declared typed premise beside the corpus pins because the admitted archive verifies nothing about them.

## 1. The problem, exactly

`T11-111` states that the standing classifier "enters `ConstructorContinuityObserved` for rows observed by the accepted archive's validated continuity report and `NativeAcceptanceObserved { identity }` for the sponsorless positive row on the accepted target identity, only from present constructor material whose acceptance is `Established` under the variable schedule with matching readback identity" (`plans/backlog.md:1304`). Three of those clauses are contradicted by the tree, and one is unimplementable because the production path it names is closed.

The first contradiction is the plural. `constructor_records` attributes every entry of a validated continuity report to one row — the positive section's `sponsorless` (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs:1865–1866`) — and `present_material_associates_exact_records_without_moving_standings` asserts that attribution for every record paired with an entry (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs:2381–2385`). The report's other associated subjects are the continuity mutants, and each of those is an experimental subject (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs:397`), a wrapper whose standing is `SubjectStanding::Experimental` and whose `counts_as_evidence` is false (`packages/vectors/src/subject.rs:41`, `:125–127`). There is therefore no set of rows a continuity report observes beyond the one row its entries name.

The second contradiction is the bucket itself. Exactly one standing stands per row (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs:1104`), and the sponsorless row's question is an acceptance: §23's seventh conjunct is "one sponsorless positive control is accepted by a real target" at (`gate:guide14-exec:exit`), and the Wave-9 closure records that conjunct as conditionally met on the accepted run, with `T11-111` owning "entry of that acceptance into the row classifier" at (`sec:phase6:wave9-findings`). A row that takes the acceptance cannot also take the continuity observation, so the two clauses of `T11-111` compete for the same row.

The third contradiction is the gate on present material. `derive_maturity_evidence_plan_with` reads the historical corpus itself, unconditionally, and mints `NativeDeclaredBoundaryObserved` from it without any supplied material (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs:1757–1774`); the corpus is the admitted immutable archive, not a caller's premise. The accepted corpus is admitted on the same terms (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_corpus.rs:1526–1534`), so routing its acceptance through supplied material would treat one admitted archive differently from the other for no stated reason.

The unimplementable path is the established acceptance. `ValidatedMaturityContinuity::acceptance_obligation` is a `const fn` returning `Outstanding` with both routes, for every projection and every source (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_continuity.rs:2023–2031`); `MaturityProjectionInput` carries no acceptance field (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_continuity.rs:203–216`); `assemble_maturity_continuity_report` sets the report's acceptance to the first source's obligation (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_continuity_report.rs:1263–1273`, `:1297`). No production path produces an `Established` report, so the `Established` branch of `constructor_records` (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs:1844–1856`) is reachable only from a hand-built value, and the end-to-end binary asserts the outcome directly: `accepted_archive_projection_and_report_keep_acceptance_outstanding` requires `Outstanding` for all three sources, the accepted archive included, and requires the rendered bytes to carry the line `acceptance outstanding` (`packages/vectors/tests/maturity_continuity.rs:611–634`).

That last assertion is where the tree says something the tree contradicts. The accepted archive's own native evidence carries `MaturityAcceptanceObligation::Established` — `from_transcript` mints it when the planner's schedule is `VariableMetadata` and the submission's observed layer is `Accepted` (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_native.rs:285–300`) — and the corpus importer binds that established value field by field to the recorded archive: accepted schedule against the pins, accepted identity against the readback's identity, witness identity, block hash and block height (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_corpus.rs:1325–1356`). A continuity report assembled over those same bytes prints `acceptance outstanding`. The projector, not the corpus, is what loses the fact.

The single production producer of `Established` checks what an acceptance claim must carry. `check_submission` decodes the submitted bytes, recomputes the identity as the double SHA-256 of `encode_without_witness()` and the witness identity as the double SHA-256 of the complete encoding, and refuses unless the response's accepted identity, the readback's transaction identity, the readback's raw transaction bytes and the readback's witness identity all agree (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_native.rs:614–655`). The construction of those identities is proved against the reference implementation by `the_reference_txid_is_the_first_party_witness_stripped_digest` (`packages/target-elements-conformance/src/tests/reference_oracle_tests.rs:177–206`); the same two-line computation is repeated privately in `txid_for` (`packages/vectors/src/live_corpus_native_v2_r7.rs:1446–1452`) and in the planner at `packages/vectors/src/maturity_native.rs:630`, and the transaction crate publishes no identity function of its own.

The classifier's declared-boundary rule is already schedule-indexed, and the matrix's is not. `expected_layer` maps the sponsorless subject under the whole-metadata schedule to `RelayPolicyRejection` and under the variable schedule to `Accepted`, under the doc sentence "The matrix boundary declared before the submission is executed" (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_native.rs:53–70`). `MaturityNativeEvidence::from_transcript` computes its standing from that map, so the accepted archive stands `AnsweredAtDeclaredBoundary` exactly as the refused one does (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_native.rs:279–284`, `:314–318`), and the importer requires that standing for both archives (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_corpus.rs:1303–1308`). The matrix carries one declaration for the row — the positive row `sponsorless` bound to the relay-policy layer with the target as its carrier (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_safety.rs:1444–1448`) — and the classifier's own special case re-implements the comparison a second time, by hard-coding a test against `EvidenceBoundary::RelayPolicyRejection` (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs:1763`). One rule with two implementations is the failure `from_native_refusal`'s doc names as the reason wrong-boundary rows once stood as answered (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs:1229–1237`).

The environment the acceptance holds in has no typed carrier anywhere. The executor's development node ran with `-minrelaytxfee=0` and `-blockmintxfee=0`; the disclosure is one doc sentence on `maturity_variable_run_of_record` (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_corpus.rs:1518`) and two argument strings in the driver (`scripts/elements-native-executor.py:2199–2200`). `MaturityCorpusPins` carries schedule, file names, file sizes, three addresses, the sponsorless expectation, a cargo path, a framework revision and a funding count, and no fee field (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_corpus.rs:140–150`). `MaturityReportFacts` carries sixteen fields, every one of them parsed from the archive's RUN-REPORT, and no fee field (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_corpus.rs:327–343`). The accepted archive's RUN-REPORT is a fixed-key grammar with no fee line (`packages/vectors/fixtures/maturity-variable-run-of-record/RUN-REPORT`), and the archive is immutable: its four file names, sizes and digests are pinned by `VARIABLE_PINS` (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_corpus.rs:266`). A fee floor therefore cannot be parsed from the evidence that exists; it can only be declared.

## 3. Decisions

**D1. `ConstructorContinuityObserved` answers no row of the current matrix, and the bucket stands at zero by argument rather than by absence.** The merits are a conjunction the tree fixes on three sides. One standing stands per row (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs:1104`); the only row a validated continuity report's entries are attributed to is §16.1's sponsorless positive row (`:1865–1866`), whose question is an acceptance and whose answer D2 assigns; every other subject the report carries is an experimental subject and is never countable evidence (`packages/vectors/src/subject.rs:41`, `:125–127`); and the one row whose question names the constructor projection at all is §16.1's `accepted-target-transaction-with-semantic-constructor-root-and-recovery-projections-all-matching`, which expects all four terms to match (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_safety.rs:1468–1472`, `:1308–1325`) while the root-history and public-recovery terms have no producer — the root-history registry stands outstanding under `BranchIndexedHistoryIsNotProjectable` (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs:1785–1786`) and §16.1's own `public-successor-recovery` row carries `PublicRecoveryAnsweredByAnotherWave`. Answering that four-term row from a continuity report alone is exactly what §1.14 forbids at (`rule:guide14-exec:evidence-separation`), what §23's fifteenth conjunct keeps apart at (`gate:guide14-exec:exit`), and what ruling 72 at (`rule:phase6:wave9-rulings`) settled when it gave continuity its own report rather than a share of safety's. The consequence is that `T11-111`'s first clause has no referent: the classifier enters `ConstructorContinuityObserved` for no row, the bucket's zero is a recounted fact rather than a missing producer, and the standing keeps its vocabulary place on §14.2's authority exactly as `RootHistoryObserved` and `PublicRecoveryObserved` do.

**D2. The sponsorless positive row's standing becomes `NativeAcceptanceObserved`, displacing `NativeDeclaredBoundaryObserved`.** The row asks whether a sponsorless positive control is accepted by a real target, which is §23's seventh conjunct at (`gate:guide14-exec:exit`); the accepted archive answers it, and the Wave-9 closure assigns that entry to `T11-111` at (`sec:phase6:wave9-findings`). The historical observation it displaces is weaker on the row's own question: `NativeDeclaredBoundaryObserved`'s doc says in terms that "reaching the relay boundary leaves the accepted positive control outstanding" (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs:1151–1152`), so the two standings are not rivals of equal strength but a question and its answer. Both members are answered (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs:1257–1269`), so the displacement is census-neutral in `answered()`: `native_declared_boundary_observed` moves 1 to 0, `native_acceptance_observed` moves 0 to 1, `answered()` holds at 41, `native_run_required` holds at 42, and the six-bucket partition still sums to 206. The historical refusal stays recorded where it is admitted — in `maturity_run_of_record`, in its own pins and detail, and in the negative-half register's doc sentence — rather than in a row standing that the acceptance has superseded.

**D3. The classifier accepts only an acceptance anchored to the pinned accepted archive, not any established report.** An arbitrary caller can build a `MaturityContinuityReport` and an experimental subject, and can therefore present an established obligation that no admitted archive backs; `CanonicalSubject::admit` is crate-private precisely so that minting countable evidence is the validated plan's act and not a caller's (`packages/vectors/src/subject.rs:60–75`), and `ExperimentalSubject::observe` is open to anyone while conferring nothing (`:107–123`). Admitting any established report would let an infrastructure artifact become a target verdict, which is the category error Wave 8's third exit excludes and which ruling 73 cites by name at (`rule:phase6:wave9-rulings`). The anchor is therefore the admitted corpus itself: the classifier reads `maturity_variable_run_of_record()` and takes its evidence's obligation, so the acceptance enters the plan on the same footing as the historical refusal already does at `packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs:1757–1774`, with `MATURITY_VARIABLE_RUN_ADDRESS` and `MATURITY_VARIABLE_RUN_SCHEDULE` naming what was admitted. Supplied constructor material stays what its own derivation doc calls it — an independently supplied premise for a comparison (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs:1718–1722`) — and supplies no standing, which is also what keeps `assert_standings_equal` true of the absent-material and present-material plans.

**D4. No boundary declaration changes, because the declared-boundary rule for this row is `expected_layer`, and the classifier reads that rule instead of restating it.** `expected_layer` is public, is documented as "the matrix boundary declared before the submission is executed", and returns the relay-policy layer for the whole-metadata schedule and the accepted layer for the variable one (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_native.rs:53–70`); `from_transcript` computes `AnsweredAtDeclaredBoundary` from it for both archives (`:279–284`), and the corpus importer requires that standing of both (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_corpus.rs:1303–1308`). The classifier's special case restates the comparison by hard-coding one layer (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs:1763`), and that second implementation is what a schedule-dependent row breaks; replacing it with the archive's own `AnsweredAtDeclaredBoundary` gives the rule one implementation again. The matrix row keeps its relay-policy declaration (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_safety.rs:1444–1448`): the census reads that value only through `requires_target_execution`, which is true of the relay-policy and accepted layers alike (`packages/vectors/src/matrix.rs:273–283`), so retyping it would move no figure while costing the whole-metadata declaration its home. **D5. **D6. The projector takes an acceptance claim and verifies it against the bytes it projected, refusing a mismatch through one new refusal variant.** The claim belongs on `MaturityProjectionInput`, whose doc already says "caller-supplied operands, with no caller-supplied verdict" (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_continuity.rs:202`); the verdict stays the projector's because the projector recomputes it. Four comparisons make the check a recomputation rather than a second source of truth: the claimed readback bytes equal the submitted bytes the projector decoded, the claimed identity equals the double SHA-256 of the decoded transaction's `encode_without_witness()`, the claimed witness identity equals the double SHA-256 of the complete encoding, and the claimed schedule equals the bundle's retained schedule. The first three are exactly what `check_submission` already performs on the capture side (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_native.rs:625–643`) and what `compare_payload` re-binds at admission (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_corpus.rs:1325–1356`), so a disagreement means the claim did not come from the bytes in hand. The identity computation is published once, as a named function beside the projector rather than a third private copy of the two-line expression that stands at `packages/vectors/src/maturity_native.rs:630` and `packages/vectors/src/live_corpus_native_v2_r7.rs:1451`, with `the_reference_txid_is_the_first_party_witness_stripped_digest` as the evidence that the construction is the target's (`packages/target-elements-conformance/src/tests/reference_oracle_tests.rs:177–206`). The new variant's consumers are countable: `MaturityContinuityRefusal` is `#[non_exhaustive]` (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_continuity.rs:413–415`), so every match outside the vectors crate already carries a wildcard and gains nothing; inside the crate exactly one match is exhaustive — `continuity_refusal_name`, 27 arms, one call site (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_report.rs:1369–1398`, `:1434`) — and it gains one arm; `MaturityContinuityRefusal::diagnostics` wildcards its tail and takes the new variant silently (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_continuity.rs:527–541`), which is correct here because an acceptance mismatch reaches no commitment-binding comparison and has no projection diagnostics to retain; and every other in-crate mention constructs a variant or matches one pattern with a rest binding (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs:445`, `:2232`; `packages/vectors/src/maturity_report.rs:2138`; `packages/vectors/tests/maturity_continuity.rs:647–721`).

**D7. The report's acceptance is a join over its sources, and the validator's all-sources-agree rule weakens to "outstanding, or equal to the report's established".** The current rule takes the first source's obligation and then requires every source to equal it (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_continuity_report.rs:1263–1273`, `:1348–1349`), which is sound only while every source returns the same constant. Under D6 it stops being sound immediately and reachably: the end-to-end binary assembles one report over three sources — a node-free submit-ready candidate, the refused archive and the accepted archive — and only the third can carry an established obligation (`packages/vectors/tests/maturity_continuity.rs:611–634`), so the very first report the tree assembles after D6 mixes the two states. The join is the rule that reads that mixture correctly: an outstanding source is silent about acceptance rather than contradicting it, so the report is established when at least one source is established and no two established sources disagree, and outstanding otherwise; the validator then requires each source to be outstanding or equal to the report's established value, and requires the report's value to equal the recomputed join. The disagreement arm — two established sources carrying different obligations — is unreachable in the current tree, because the established obligation is minted only by `from_transcript` under the variable schedule with an accepted submission (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_native.rs:285–300`) and exactly one accepted archive is admitted (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_corpus.rs:1526–1534`). The test that makes it reachable is a second established source in the assembler's input: the admitted accepted archive replayed a second time under a mutated identity, with the assembler required to refuse rather than to choose. That test is named in bite 2 so the arm is not written without a case.

**D8. `completeness` keeps its constant, and its doc stops resting on the absent readback.** `MaturitySafetyCompleteness::PartialRequiredRowsOutstanding` is correct after D6 and D7 for a reason the tree already counts: 42 rows stand `NativeRunRequired` and 15 stand `ReportLayerRequired`, so `every_required_row_is_answered()` is false (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_evidence.rs:1469–1477`, asserted at `:3294`). What changes is the justification printed beside it. The accessor's doc says "Partial while accepted readback is absent" (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_continuity_report.rs:575`) and the assembler's doc says "All possible input lengths remain partial: this input vocabulary contains no accepted readback with target-computed identity" (`:1279–1280`); both sentences are false of a report whose source carries a verified accepted readback, and both are restated to rest on the outstanding required rows instead. The validator's `Item::Completeness` keeps comparing against the constant (`:1361`), so the item's evidence is unchanged and only its reason moves.

**D9. The rendered bytes need no new vocabulary and no exception to the spelling rule.** The renderer already has an established arm printing `acceptance established`, `acceptance_schedule` and `acceptance_identity` (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_continuity_report.rs:2104–2117`), so D6 and D7 reach canonical bytes through a path that already exists. `canonical_bytes_have_no_accepted_or_observed_standing_spelling` forbids the capitalised standing words in those bytes (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_continuity_report.rs:3201–3212`), and every line the established arm writes is lowercase, so the test holds unchanged over an established report; the same holds for the end-to-end binary's two spelling assertions (`packages/vectors/tests/maturity_continuity.rs:633–634`). What the end-to-end binary changes is the acceptance disposition it asserts, not the spelling discipline it enforces.

## 4. Open questions

**OQ1. Whether the ten remaining `AcceptanceAwaitsRelayAdmissibility` rows retype to the accepted-transaction layer.** The reason's own text says "A row whose answer is an acceptance has two routes to one and neither has landed" (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_safety.rs:225–239`), and `MaturityRowBoundary`'s doc states the discipline that decides this: "a test asserts which rows carry which reason, so a reason that stopped applying — because the wave that owned it landed its evidence — fails here rather than ageing quietly into a claim nobody rechecks" (`:212–217`). One route landed: the constant-elision witness is ratified at (`[ADR025-dec:relay-witness:constant-elision]`) and its track closed at (`sec:phase6:route-closure`), and one run under it was accepted. For retyping: acceptance is reachable, so the ten rows are rows waiting on a run of their own case, which is what `NativeRunRequired` means; the figures move to `native_run_required` 52, `outstanding_under_typed_non_answer` 55 and register 52, and the partition still sums to 206. Against retyping: two of the ten are `sponsored-without-change` and `sponsored-with-change-where-supported`, whose blocker is the sponsored arm rather than relay admissibility, so a blanket retyping would give them a boundary their case cannot arrive at — the failure `SponsorArmAwaitsRegionScoping` exists to prevent (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_safety.rs:246–248`); and the remaining eight enlarge the waiting register by eight rows for evidence no bite in this study produces. The question is which of the eleven rows the landed route actually unblocks, and it is a matrix question rather than a classifier question, so it is not decided here.

**OQ2. Whether §16.1's last row is one row or four.** The row names the semantic, constructor, root-history and public-recovery projections together and expects all four to match; the term vocabulary keeps them apart precisely because "a row that expects one of them to differ is making a different claim from a row that expects another" (`packages/vectors/src/maturity_safety.rs:678–680`). For splitting: four rows, one per term, would give each report exactly one row to answer, which is what `ConstructorContinuityObserved`, `RootHistoryObserved` and `PublicRecoveryObserved` are shaped for, and would let the continuity report answer its own row without answering anybody else's by implication. Against splitting: it changes the matrix's denominator from 206, against which every census figure and the register are pinned, and §16 is transcribed rather than designed here — a row the guide states as one conjunction is not this workspace's to divide without the guide moving first. The question is whether the four-term row is a transcription of §16 or a shorthand for four, and answering it needs §16's text rather than the classifier's.
